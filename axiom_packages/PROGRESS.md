### 6. zfc_set_theory v1.0.0

**Mathematical Theory**: Zermelo-Fraenkel Set Theory with the Axiom of Choice (ZFC) — the standard axiomatic foundation for all of modern mathematics. All mathematical objects are encoded as pure, hereditarily well-founded sets in the von Neumann cumulative hierarchy V = ∪_{α∈Ord} V_α. ZFC is formalized in first-order logic with a single binary predicate ∈ (set membership).

  - nLab: ZFC

- **Primitive Notions**: set membership (∈); all objects are pure sets (hereditarily well-founded); no urelements. Equality is defined by extensionality (same elements → same set).
- **Core Axioms**: 9 axioms:

### 7. intuitionistic_propositional_logic v1.0.0

**Mathematical Theory**: Intuitionistic propositional logic (IPL), also known as constructive propositional logic. Formalized originally by Arend Heyting (1930) to provide a formal basis for L.E.J. Brouwer's programme of intuitionism. Unlike classical propositional logic (CPL), IPL rejects the law of excluded middle (φ ∨ ¬φ) and double negation elimination (¬¬φ → φ). The key constructive property is the disjunction property: if ⊢ φ ∨ ψ, then either ⊢ φ or ⊢ ψ.

- **References**:
  - Heyting, A. (1930). "Die formalen Regeln der intuitionistischen Logik I, II, III." Sitzungsberichte der Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften. pp. 42-56, 57-71, 158-169.
  - Wikipedia: Intuitionistic logic
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitionistic_logic
  - nLab: Intuitionistic logic
    https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/intuitionistic+logic
  - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Intuitionistic Logic
    https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-intuitionistic/
  - Troelstra, A.S. & van Dalen, D. (1988). "Constructivism in Mathematics: An Introduction" (2 vols). North-Holland.
  - Rybakov, V.V. (1997). "Admissibility of Logical Inference Rules." Studies in Logic, Vol. 136. Elsevier.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admissible_rule
  - Wikipedia: Heyting algebra
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heyting_algebra
  - Statman, R. (1979). "Intuitionistic propositional logic is polynomial-space complete." TCS, 9(1): 67-72.
    https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(79)90006-9
- **Primitive Notions**: propositional variable, ⊥ (falsum / absurdity); connectives → (implication), ∧ (conjunction), ∨ (disjunction), ⊥ — ALL primitive in IPL (unlike CPL where they are interdefinable). ¬φ is defined as φ → ⊥.
- **Core Axioms**: 9 Hilbert-style axiom schemas + 1 inference rule (Heyting, 1930):
  - THEN-1: φ → (ψ → φ) [K combinator / Weakening]
  - THEN-2: (φ → (ψ → χ)) → ((φ → ψ) → (φ → χ)) [S combinator / Distribution]
  - AND-1: φ ∧ ψ → φ, AND-2: φ ∧ ψ → ψ, AND-3: φ → (ψ → (φ ∧ ψ))
  - OR-1: φ → φ ∨ ψ, OR-2: ψ → φ ∨ ψ, OR-3: (φ → χ) → ((ψ → χ) → (φ ∨ ψ → χ))
  - FALSE (EFQ): ⊥ → φ
  - Rule: Modus Ponens (MP)
- **Constraint Templates**: 51 in 8 groups:
  - Group I (9): Core axiom schemata (K, S, AND-1/2/3, OR-1/2/3, FALSE)
  - Group II (1): Modus Ponens (sole inference rule)
  - Group III (5): Primitive connectives (→, ∧, ∨, ⊥, ¬)
  - Group IV (5): Derived connectives (↔, ⊕, NAND, NOR, ⊤)
  - Group V (14): Derived inference rules (hypothetical syllogism, modus tollens, disjunctive syllogism, conjunction intro/elim, disjunction intro/elim, biconditional intro/elim, DN intro, reductio, deduction theorem)
  - Group VI (8): Propositional identities (De Morgan OR full, De Morgan AND weakened, idempotence, commutativity, associativity)
  - Group VII (4): IPL-CPL bridge (Glivenko theorem, Gödel-Gentzen negative translation + soundness, Kuroda translation)
  - Group VIII (5): Forward contrapositive, exportation, importation, proof by cases, triple negation reduction
- **Known Unconstructible Problems**: 6 (intuitionistic tautology checking/PSPACE-complete, satisfiability/PSPACE-complete, equivalence checking/PSPACE-complete, unification problem/undecidable, admissibility in IPL/undecidable, intermediate logic axiomatization/undecidable)
- **Bottom Geometry**: intuitionistic_propositional_heyting
- **Negation Encoding**: implication_to_falsum (¬φ ≡ φ → ⊥)
- **Contradiction Behavior**: explosion_principle — EFQ (⊥→φ) IS an axiom in Heyting's system; constructivity manifests in the absence of LEM/DNE/Peirce, not in blocking explosion
- **Relationship to classical_propositional_logic**: IPL ⊂ CPL. Adding LEM or DNE to IPL recovers CPL. Glivenko's theorem (1929): φ is a CPL tautology iff ¬¬φ is an IPL theorem. The Gödel-Gentzen negative translation faithfully embeds CPL into IPL.
- **Relationship to boolean_algebra**: The Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of IPL is a Heyting algebra (generalizing Boolean algebra). Every Boolean algebra is a Heyting algebra; adding LEM to a Heyting algebra yields a Boolean algebra.
- **Curry-Howard**: IPL corresponds to simply-typed λ-calculus with product and sum types. THEN-1 = K, THEN-2 = S. The absence of Peirce's Law corresponds to the absence of call/cc.

### 8. point_set_topology v1.0.0

**Mathematical Theory**: Point-set topology (general topology), formalized via the open set axioms. A topological space (X, τ) consists of a set X and a collection τ ⊆ P(X) of "open sets" satisfying three axioms: the empty set and X are open, arbitrary unions of open sets are open, and finite intersections of open sets are open.

### 28. linear_logic v1.0.0

**Mathematical Theory**: Linear logic — introduced by Jean-Yves Girard in 1987, a refinement of classical and intuitionistic logic that treats formulas as resources rather than truth values. The central insight is that logical connectives split into multiplicative and additive variants when structural rules (contraction and weakening) are restricted. Linear logic provides a fine-grained analysis of proof structure and has profound applications in computer science (linear type systems, concurrency, quantum computing, resource management).

- **References**:
  - Girard, J.-Y. (1987). "Linear Logic." *Theoretical Computer Science*, 50(1): 1-102. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(87)90045-4
  - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Linear Logic
    https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-linear/
  - Abramsky, S. (1993). "Computational Interpretations of Linear Logic." *Theoretical Computer Science*, 111(1-2): 3-57. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(93)90180-R
  - Andreoli, J.-M. (1992). "Logic Programming with Focusing Proofs in Linear Logic." *Journal of Logic and Computation*, 2(3): 297-347. https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/2.3.297
  - Wadler, P. (1993). "A Taste of Linear Logic." MFCS 1993. https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-57182-5_24
  - nLab: Linear logic
    https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/linear+logic
- **Primitive Notions**: Linear proposition (formula treated as resource), linear sequent Γ ⊢ Δ (multisets of formulas), involutive negation ⊥ (De Morgan dualities with all connectives)
- **Core Connectives** (4 groups):
  - Multiplicative: ⊗ (tensor/multiplicative conjunction), ⅋ (par/multiplicative disjunction), 1 (multiplicative unit left), ⊥ (multiplicative unit right)
  - Additive: & (with/additive conjunction), ⊕ (plus/additive disjunction), ⊤ (additive unit left), 0 (additive unit right)
  - Exponential (modal): ! (of-course/bang, enables contraction/weakening on left), ? (why-not, enables contraction/weakening on right)
  - Linear implication: A ⊸ B ≡ A⊥ ⅋ B (resource-consuming), A ⇒ B ≡ !A ⊸ B (intuitionistic, reusable)
- **Structural Rules**: Contraction and weakening are restricted to formulas marked with exponentials (! on left, ? on right). Exchange is always allowed (multiset contexts).
- **Sequent Calculus Rules** (Girard 1987):
  - Identity: init (B ⊢ B), cut (Γ₁ ⊢ B,Δ₁  Γ₂,B ⊢ Δ₂  ⇒  Γ₁,Γ₂ ⊢ Δ₁,Δ₂)
  - Negation: ⊥L (Γ ⊢ B,Δ  ⇒  Γ,B⊥ ⊢ Δ), ⊥R (Γ,B ⊢ Δ  ⇒  Γ ⊢ B⊥,Δ)

  - Additive: 0L, ⊤R, &L (i=1,2), &R, ⊕L, ⊕R (i=1,2)
  - Exponential: !W, !C, !D, !R, ?W, ?C, ?D, ?L
- **Key Fragments**:
  - MLL (Multiplicative Linear Logic): ⊗, ⅋, 1, ⊥, ⊥ — NP-complete
  - MALL (Multiplicative Additive Linear Logic): MLL + &, ⊕, ⊤, 0 — PSPACE-complete
  - MELL (Multiplicative Exponential Linear Logic): MLL + !, ? — undecidable (open problem)
  - Full linear logic: MALL + exponentials — undecidable
- **Constraint Templates**: 53 in 9 groups:
  - Group I (3): Identity & structural rules (init, cut, exchange)
  - Group II (8): Negation (⊥L, ⊥R, double negation involution, 6 De Morgan dualities)

  - Group V (6): Additive connectives (&L, &R, ⊕L, ⊕R, ⊤R, 0L)
  - Group VI (8): Exponential connectives (!W, !C, !D, !R, ?W, ?C, ?D, ?L)
  - Group VII (4): Exponential equivalences (! distributes over ⊗, !⊤≡1, ? distributes over ⅋, ?0≡⊥)
  - Group VIII (3): Comonoid laws for ! (to linear, comultiplication, counit)
  - Group IX (10): Derived constructors & encodings (intuitionistic implication, classical connectives, excluded middle multiplicative, focused proof system, linear-to-intuitionistic translation, resource management patterns)
- **Known Unconstructible Problems**: 10:
  1. Provability in full propositional linear logic — undecidable (Lincoln, Mitchell, Scedrov, Shankar 1992)
  2. Provability in MELL — **open problem** (long-standing, marked as green_verified=false)
  3. Proof net normalization for full linear logic — undecidable
  4. Type inhabitation for full linear logic — undecidable
  5. Equality of proof nets — undecidable

  7. Additive excluded middle (A ⊕ A⊥) — not provable (contrast with multiplicative A ⅋ A⊥)

- **Negation Encoding**: involutive_linear_negation — Linear negation is a primitive, involutive operation with full De Morgan dualities. It is its own dual: (A⊥)⊥ = A. Unlike classical logic (A → ⊥) or intuitionistic logic, linear negation is not defined via implication.
- **Contradiction Behavior**: constructive (blocking) — Linear logic does NOT have the explosion principle (ex falso quodlibet). From ⊥, one cannot derive arbitrary propositions. The absence of weakening means that ⊥ cannot be discarded to derive anything else. Note: In the full system with exponentials, !⊥ ⊸ A is provable (recovering a form of explosion for reusable contradictions), but ⊥ ⊸ A is NOT provable.
- **Relationship to existing packages**:
  - **intuitionistic_logic**: Linear logic refines intuitionistic logic by restricting structural rules. The intuitionistic implication A ⇒ B is encoded as !A ⊸ B. Linear logic provides a resource-sensitive interpretation of constructive proofs.
  - **classical_propositional_logic**: With exponentials, linear logic recovers classical logic: A ∨ B ≡ !(!(A ⊕ B)), A ∧ B ≡ !(A & B). The multiplicative excluded middle A ⅋ A⊥ is provable, while the additive A ⊕ A⊥ is not.
  - **simple_type_theory**: Linear type systems (in programming languages) are based on linear logic. Linear implication ⊸ corresponds to linear function types, and the ! modality corresponds to unrestricted types.
  - **category_theory**: MLL is modeled by *-autonomous categories. Linear logic provides a categorical semantics for proof theory and connects to monoidal categories.
  - **boolean_algebra**: The multiplicative conjunction ⊗ and additive conjunction & both generalize classical ∧, but with different resource semantics. Linear logic reveals the hidden resource structure in classical logic.
  - **proof_theory**: Linear logic was discovered through a semantic analysis of System F (polymorphic λ-calculus). Proof nets (Girard 1987) provide a graph-theoretic representation of proofs that eliminates bureaucracy.
- **Key Theorems & Properties**:
  - Cut elimination theorem (Girard 1987): Every proof in linear logic can be transformed into a cut-free proof.
  - Focused proof search (Andreoli 1992): Proofs can be organized into asynchronous (invertible) and synchronous (non-invertible) phases, improving proof search efficiency.
  - Phase semantics: A complete semantics for linear logic based on phase spaces (Girard 1987).
  - Curry-Howard correspondence: Linear logic proofs correspond to linear λ-calculus terms (linear types).
  - Resource interpretation: Formulas are resources that must be used exactly once (unless marked with ! or ?).
  - De Morgan dualities: (A ⊗ B)⊥ ≡ A⊥ ⅋ B⊥, (A & B)⊥ ≡ A⊥ ⊕ B⊥, (!A)⊥ ≡ ?(A⊥)
  - Multiplicative excluded middle: ⊢ A ⅋ A⊥ is provable (trivially, via init + ⅋R)
  - Additive excluded middle: ⊢ A ⊕ A⊥ is NOT provable (intuitionistic reading of disjunction)

### 37. affine_geometry v1.0.0

**Mathematical Theory**: Affine geometry — the study of geometric properties invariant under affine transformations. It is what remains of Euclidean geometry when the metric notions of distance and angle are "forgotten" (Euler 1748, Klein's Erlangen program 1872). The fundamental invariant is parallelism.
- **References**:
  - Coxeter, H.S.M. (1969). "Introduction to Geometry" (2nd ed.). Wiley.
  - Artin, E. (1957). "Geometric Algebra." Wiley. ISBN 978-1-118-16454-9.
  - Snapper, E. & Troyer, R.J. (1971). "Metric Affine Geometry." Springer-Verlag. ISBN 0-387-90036-5.
  - Berger, M. (1987). "Geometry, Vol. I & II." Springer. ISBN 3-540-11658-3.
  - Wikipedia: Affine geometry
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_geometry
  - Wikipedia: Affine space
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_space
  - nLab: Affine space
    https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/affine+space
- **Primitive Notions**: point, line, vector (displacement); incidence, parallelism, subtraction (point - point = vector), translation (point + vector)
- **Core Axioms**: 17 axioms in 6 groups:
  - I. Incidence (4 axioms) — existence/uniqueness of points, lines, affine frames
  - II. Parallelism (4 axioms) — Playfair's axiom, parallelism as equivalence relation
  - III. Vector Space (11 axioms) — displacements form a vector space (abelian group + scalar multiplication)
  - IV. Affine Structure (4 axioms) — point-vector interaction (Chasles relation, translation-subtraction compatibility)
  - V. Desargues' Theorem (1 axiom) — free in dim >= 3, required in 2D
  - VI. Pappus's Theorem (1 axiom) — implies coordinate field commutativity
- **Constraint Templates**: 50 (4 incidence + 4 parallelism + 11 vector space + 4 affine structure + 7 affine combinations + 8 affine transformations + 2 theorems + 5 affine subspaces + 4 ratio/division + 4 parallelogram + 3 projective connection)
- **Known Unconstructible Problems**: 7 (perpendicular bisector, angle trisection, circle construction, finite affine plane non-prime-power existence, non-Desarguesian classification, metric recovery, area computation)
- **Bottom Geometry**: affine_space
- **Negation Encoding**: classical_material_implication
- **Contradiction Behavior**: explosion_principle

**File**: `axiom_packages/affine_geometry.lvz`
**Test**: `tests/test_axiom_affine_geometry.c` (10 test suites)

**Relationships to existing packages**:
- **euclidean_plane**: Affine geometry is Euclidean minus distance/angle; Euclidean is affine + metric structure
- **projective_geometry**: Affine = projective minus a line at infinity; projective completion adds points at infinity
- **hyperbolic_geometry**: The Klein model lives inside projective geometry; Beltrami-Klein model uses affine structure
- **elliptic_geometry**: Elliptic geometry has no parallel lines, unlike affine
- **field_theory**: Pappus's theorem implies the coordinate field is commutative
- **group_theory**: The translation group of an affine space is an abelian group
